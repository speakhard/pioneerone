How it was made

Two people tried to make television without television.

In 2010 that was a strange thing to attempt. No network was going to buy a science-fiction drama from two people nobody had heard of, so Josh Bernhard and Bracey Smith didn’t ask one. They put the pilot out free over BitTorrent and let the audience decide whether there should be more of it.

The audience decided there should, and paid for it. Almost everyone worked for free. It took a year and a half and very nearly didn’t finish.

That argument — that you can make television without television — is settled now, and it went the way we said. It just took longer than we could personally afford to wait.

Now

The story isn’t necessarily over.

After the first season I spent about a decade developing larger versions of Pioneer One inside the film and television industry — good rooms, better writers than me, real money on the table. I learned an enormous amount. None of it got made, and by the end the thing being developed had drifted a long way from what I liked about it in the first place.

I’ve come back to it from somewhere else. Four things are actually happening:

  • A feature film. I’m writing a feature-length interpretation of the material — the same story, told once, at the size a film can hold.
  • The original six episodes. Going back through the season and working out what a proper restoration and re-release would actually involve.
  • A documentary. I’m making a documentary about how the show got made — about arguing for something that turned out to be right, years before it was obvious.
  • What continuing it would mean. Thinking seriously about the rest of the story — the part we never got to tell.

None of that is an announcement. There is no second season in production, no financing in place, and nothing has been greenlit. This is a door being opened again, which is a smaller and more honest thing than a relaunch.
