How it was made

Two people tried to make television without television.

In 2010, Josh Bernhard and Bracey Smith raised $6,000 on Kickstarter and shot a pilot. They gave it away through VODO, which distributed films over BitTorrent, because no network was going to commission a science-fiction drama from two people nobody had heard of.

It was downloaded more than two million times in a fortnight. Strangers sent $20,000 through a PayPal button on the site. That money shot the next three episodes, filmed together across one October. The last of the six went out in December 2011.

Almost everyone worked for free.

Now

The story isn’t necessarily over.

After the first season I spent about a decade developing larger versions of Pioneer One inside the film and television industry — good rooms, better writers than me, real money on the table. I learned an enormous amount. None of it got made, and by the end the thing being developed had drifted a long way from what I liked about it in the first place.

I’ve come back to it from somewhere else. Three things are actually happening:

  • A feature film. I’m writing a feature-length interpretation of the material with a friend, who would direct it.
  • The original six episodes. Going back through the season and working out what a proper restoration and re-release would actually involve.
  • What continuing it would mean. Thinking seriously about the rest of the story — the part we never got to tell.

None of that is an announcement. There is no second season in production, no financing in place, and nothing has been commissioned. This is a door being opened again, which is a smaller and more honest thing than a relaunch.
